# Calque

> **calque** (`/kælk/`, noun & verb)
> 1. *Linguistics:* A word or phrase borrowed from another language by translating its component parts literally, piece-by-piece (e.g., English *skyscraper* → Spanish *rascacielos*).
> 2. *Art & Design:* Translucent tracing paper (*papier calque*) laid over an original page to reveal and trace the structure underneath.

## Purpose & Vision
**Calque** exists for one specific purpose: **teaching English speakers how to read Spanish fluently, rapidly, and independently.** 

Most language applications treat reading, speaking, listening, and writing as a single tangled problem. They force learners through repetitive vocabulary drills, artificial gamification, and rigid grammar tables before letting them engage with real text. Calque discards that model. Reading is a decoding problem, not a production problem.

## The "Taste" of the App
The product feels like a quiet, intelligent edition of an annotated book—or a sheet of translucent tracing paper laid over a foreign page—not an arcade game. There are no streaks, cartoon mascots, leaderboards, or punitive hearts. The reward is the immediate satisfaction of decoding a foreign sentence and realizing you understood it. It respects the learner's intelligence and leverages the massive linguistic head start they already possess as an English speaker.

## Core Assumptions
1. **The Learner Speaks English**: English and Spanish share thousands of Latin-derived roots. An English speaker already recognizes 3,000+ Spanish words on Day 1 once they learn how suffix patterns shift.
2. **Reading Comprehension Precedes Output**: Recognizing what *hablábamos* means in context is exponentially easier than conjugating it from scratch under pressure. The app optimizes strictly for recognition and comprehension.
3. **Dictionary Switching Destroys Flow**: Every time a reader leaves a sentence to look up a word, their working memory resets. All disambiguation must happen inline, in milliseconds, without losing the thread of the sentence.
4. **Grammar is a Decoding Tool, Not a Curriculum**: Learners do not need to memorize 6x6 conjugation grids. They only need to recognize the high-frequency signals that change *who* did *what* and *when* (primarily 3rd-person narratives, past vs. present markers, and attached pronouns).

## Pedagogical Strategy
The learning progression moves across four deliberate stages:

1. **Pattern Unlocking (The Cognate Bridge)**: Immediately demystifying Spanish by showing how English suffixes (`-tion`, `-ty`, `-ous`, `-ly`) map directly to Spanish equivalents (`-ción`, `-dad`, `-oso`, `-mente`), while explicitly flagging "False Friends" (*falsos amigos*) that cause silent comprehension failures.
2. **Phonetic Anchoring (Subvocalization)**: Teaching the five unchanging vowel sounds and core consonant rules so the learner's internal reading voice is accurate and rhythmic from the first sentence.
3. **Scaffolded Sentence Decoding ($i + 1$)**: Moving one syntactic hurdle at a time—starting with simple Subject-Verb-Object sentences rich in cognates, then introducing post-nominal adjectives, negation, omitted subjects, and clitic pronouns.
4. **Supported Paragraph & Custom Text Reading**: Transitioning into micro-stories and user-imported texts where unknown words, verb inflections, and false cognates are transparently unpacked on demand.

## Non-Goals
- We do **not** teach conversational speaking or grade microphone pronunciation.
- We do **not** test spelling or require the user to type Spanish accents from memory.
- We do **not** require an internet connection to read, look up words, or practice.
